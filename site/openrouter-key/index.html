GuideOpenRouter key

One OpenRouter key for Claude, GPT and Gemini.

LoomWatch's agents think with AI models from many companies, and you reach them all with one key from OpenRouter. Here's how to make that key, add it to LoomWatch, and keep an eye on what it spends.

What OpenRouter is

OpenRouter is a service that gives you AI models from many companies through one account: Claude from Anthropic, GPT from OpenAI, Gemini from Google, and DeepSeek, Kimi, Qwen and hundreds more, some of them free. You pay OpenRouter for what you use, and it pays the model makers.

A key is a long secret code that lets an app use your OpenRouter account. You paste it into LoomWatch once, and every agent you build can think with any of those models.

It's one of three ways LoomWatch can pay for AI models, and the only one that reaches every company's models, free ones included. If you already have ChatGPT Plus or Pro, or Claude Max or Team, you can use that plan instead, or as well: the FAQ says how. You don't need either to use OpenRouter.

Make a key

  1. Go to openrouter.ai and sign up.
  2. Add a few dollars of credit. Some models are free, but most cost a little each time an agent uses them.
  3. Open openrouter.ai/keys, create a key and copy it. You can give it a credit limit there, so it can never spend more than you chose.

Keep your key to yourself. Anyone with it can spend your credit. If it ever leaks, delete it at openrouter.ai/keys and make a new one.

Add it to LoomWatch

  1. On Home, paste the key into OpenRouter (pay per use), one of the three ways to pay shown there. (Later you'll find it in Connections…, in the ☰ menu.)
  2. LoomWatch checks the key with OpenRouter and shows how much credit is left on it.

The key stays on your computer: in the Mac's Keychain, or in Windows Credential Manager. Your agents never see it. It isn't in their commands, their folders, the team file or the run history.

To take it out again, click Remove key on OpenRouter (pay per use) in Connections…. To stop it working anywhere, delete it at openrouter.ai/keys too.

What a run costs

OpenRouter charges your key each time an agent uses a model, at that model's price. Prices differ a lot from one model to the next, and a bigger job uses more. After every run, its receipt shows exactly what it cost, as OpenRouter reported it, so you always know where the money went.

Free models: good for trying, not for private work

The model picker marks the free ones Free. They are a fine way to try things out, but they change often, and some let their maker learn from what you send. Keep private work for a model you pay for.

Different models in one team

Each agent picks its own model, so one team can use several. New agents start on Claude Opus 5.5. An agent that checks the work starts on another company's model, so the work is read by a model that didn't write it.

A team can research with Claude, check with GPT and draft with a free model, all on the one key. The picker suggests a model for each agent, and you can search the rest. If you've also connected a ChatGPT or Claude plan, the picker shows its models in a section of their own, so you always see how a model is paid.

What is sent where

LoomWatch runs on your computer and keeps your teams and their history there. What an agent works on (its instructions, your request, and the files and pages it reads) goes to OpenRouter and to the company whose model it uses, as with any AI service. LoomWatch running on your computer doesn't make the AI models run there too.
